Featured Work

Arizona’s Happy Place

  • Brand Identity
  • Logo Design

For Arizona’s Happy Place, a podcast about finding your “happy place,” whether through mental health, physical health or community, I redesigned the show’s logo as a system built around a rising Arizona sun shaped like a cactus plant, along with a warm color palette and concepts for merch stickers.

Role
Brand Designer
Deliverables
Primary & Secondary Logos · Logomark · Color Palette · Merch Sticker Concepts
Tools
Adobe Illustrator · Figma
Year
2025

Logo Design

The challenge

The host created the podcast’s first logo by setting the show’s name over a photo. Building on that start, I developed a dedicated mark and a full logo system that could stay recognizable at small sizes and reproduce cleanly across formats.

Explorations

I developed two directions for the mark: a rising Arizona sun and a cactus, which also worked as a repeating pattern. Both paired the mark with the same “Arizona’s Happy Place” wordmark.

The host chose the sun, which was also my favorite.

The logo system

The final mark is a rising Arizona sun shaped like a cactus plant, built from angled planes of terra cotta and burnt orange. It sits above a spiked wordmark set in Modula Round OT. I chose the Modula family for its Western style, then modified it, reducing the height of the spikes so the words read more clearly.

  • A primary logo in four color versions, for dark and light backgrounds
  • A wordmark-only secondary logo in black and white
  • The sun as a standalone logomark

Color and Applications

Color

The palette pairs six warm and earthy colors (Terra Cotta, Burnt Orange, Red Ochre, Winter Sky, Bronze Green and Honey) with two neutrals, Star White and Black Bean. Each color is specified with a Pantone number for consistent printing.

Merch stickers

To show how the identity could extend into merch, I designed example stickers: a half-circle badge that sets the sun mark under the line “Find Your Happy Place,” echoing the show’s message that you can find yours anywhere, in colorways that mix terra cotta, burnt orange and black bean.

Reflection

Looking back, I wish I had delivered usage guidelines alongside the logo files. Without them, the wrong files ended up on some of the show’s touchpoints, and the logo appears cropped. Clear guidance on which version to use where, and how much space to leave around the mark, would have helped the identity carry through as intended.

I put that lesson into practice on later identity work, like the logo user guide I created for Cassie Howell.
