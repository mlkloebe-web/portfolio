Cinema Captioning

  • Accessibility Research
  • UX Design

For my graduate independent study at Arizona State University, I’m exploring how captioning could be made more accessible and usable for Deaf and hard-of-hearing moviegoers in the U.S.

Year

About the Study

The question

How might cinema captioning be designed to provide a better experience for Deaf and hard-of-hearing moviegoers while working within the practical constraints of theatrical exhibition?

The context

Movie theaters have different technical, spatial and operational considerations than personal viewing environments like streaming platforms. Any potential solution in theatrical environments needs to consider not only the captions themselves, but how viewers access and interact with them during an exhibition.

This project explores that problem through research and design.

Research & Design

Areas of focus

Understanding the viewing experience

The research phase focuses on understanding the existing captioning experience and the challenges that can affect accessibility in theatrical environments:

  • Existing cinema captioning methods
  • Accessibility considerations for Deaf and hard-of-hearing audiences
  • The relationship between captions and the cinematic image
  • Practical constraints within movie theaters
  • Opportunities for improving the viewing experience

Prototyping

Translating research into design concepts

The project will explore potential solutions through low- and high-fidelity prototypes, which will allow me to examine how different approaches affect the relationship between the viewer, captions and the film.

Shape the Study

This study is currently taking shape, and I want it shaped by the people it’s for. If you’re Deaf or hard of hearing and have experiences with captioning at the movies, good or bad, I’d love to hear from you. I’m also eager to connect with people who work in theatrical exhibition or accessibility. Research begins spring 2027.
