International Production Design Week

  • Brand Design
  • Graphic Design
  • Social Media

International Production Design Week (IPDW) is a professional initiative connected to the production design community, and one of the flagship events of the Production Designers Collective (PDC). Its original brand identity, including the logo and color system, was created by Estudio Cru in 2023. For 2025, I collaborated with PDC leadership to update it with a new type system, a reduced color palette and new graphic forms, then carried the refreshed identity into templates and assets for digital communications.

Brand Refresh

The challenge

IPDW already had an established identity from Estudio Cru, including its logo and color system. For 2025, the goal was to update that identity so it could work across different communications and formats while staying recognizable.

IPDW is also a grassroots event. Instead of just one central team, it’s organized by production design guilds and associations around the world, so the identity’s assets had to be usable by organizers in many countries. Even the typeface needed to work for organizers everywhere.

The 2025 update

Refining an established visual language

Working with PDC co-founder Inbal Weinberg, we updated the identity in three areas:

  • A new type system
  • A reduced color palette
  • New graphic forms

Why Inter

The PDC chose Inter for IPDW’s new type system because it suits an event run by organizers around the world. It’s released under the SIL Open Font License, so any guild can use it for free in print, on the web or in software, with no regional or seat limits. It’s available through Google Fonts, so organizers can install it or load it on a website without hosting the files themselves. And it supports Latin, Cyrillic, Greek and Vietnamese scripts, so organizers can set event names and copy in their own languages.

Graphic forms

A divided form, inspired by shadow play

The PDC’s concept for the new graphic forms was a divided shape reminiscent of shadow play, reflecting the relationship between elements in physical space and their expression on screen. The forms celebrate production design’s role in uniting reality and illusion to create the worlds we see in film and television. After Inbal designed the first forms, I expanded them into a larger set.

Sketching experiments with divided forms on paper and designing them in Adobe Illustrator was my favorite part of the project.

Organizers worked the forms into their own materials, including Czech Production Design (Česká filmová scénografie) and Production Design Greece.

The media kit

I designed templates and graphic assets for IPDW’s 2025 media kit, so organizers didn’t have to design each piece from scratch. We shared everything with 29 guilds and associations through a single Dropbox folder: the templates, the identity’s assets and the Inter font files.

Organizers could use the designs as they were or build their own from the same type, colors and forms. That’s what kept the system flexible: events in different countries and languages could look like their own while still reading as part of one IPDW.

Social media coordination

I ran IPDW’s Instagram account, @productiondesignweek (opens in a new tab), for both the inaugural edition in 2023 and the 2025 edition. For the first IPDW, I created the social graphics working from Estudio Cru’s original identity. In 2025, I brought the refreshed identity into IPDW’s own channel.

Outcome

IPDW 2025 ran from October 17 to 26, with 209 events in 24 countries hosted by guilds, associations, schools and studios around the world.

Reflection

IPDW gave me an opportunity to approach branding as a system of connected decisions rather than a single visual artifact.

The project strengthened my experience in evolving an existing identity so it can extend across digital communications, and reinforced my interest in the relationship between visual systems and communication.
