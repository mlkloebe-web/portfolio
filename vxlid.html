Vxlid Magazine

  • Visual Identity
  • Social Media

Vxlid is a queer satirical publication serving up The Onion-level absurdity with a queer bent. I designed its visual identity, from the logo and color palette to the type system and brand guidelines, along with the Instagram templates the social-first publication uses to publish its stories.

Visual Identity

The approach

Satire works best when it looks like the real thing. I gave Vxlid the visual language of a serious publication so the headlines could do the joking, while bright color and a playful logo hint that nothing here is quite what it seems.

Logo

The logotype sets the name in Helvetica Bold, with each letter in a different brand color. A shortened “Vx.” mark works as a flexible shorthand across digital formats, and black and white versions cover monochrome contexts where the full-color logo can’t be used.

Color

The palette pairs a near-black and a warm off-white with seven colors: red, pink, dark blue, soft blue, purple, gold and tan. Each letter of the logotype takes one of them, so the logo itself introduces the palette.

Typography

Deadpan sans, literary serif

Helvetica is the primary typeface. Nothing says “serious business” like the world’s most overused sans serif, and its deadpan neutrality gives the publication room to be ridiculous. Libre Baskerville balances it with a literary quality, a nod to traditional editorial publishing, and adds variety and readability to body copy.

  • Helvetica Bold for headlines, titles, pull quotes and key statements
  • Helvetica Regular for captions and supporting text
  • Libre Baskerville Regular for body copy and some headings

Instagram is Vxlid’s only platform for now, so I set up the type system with room to grow: the body copy styles are ready if the publication expands into longer-form editorial.

Applications

Instagram templates

Vxlid is social-first: Instagram is its only platform, so the templates are where the identity lives day to day. I designed reusable templates that turn each story into a post, balancing readability with a distinct point of view through typography, hierarchy and image placement: a full-bleed photo, the headline set in Libre Baskerville, a Helvetica byline and the logo.

The straight-faced editorial format lets headlines like “7 Non-Binary Teens Open Up About Sand” land on their own. Vxlid’s team, led by editor-in-chief V Marks, uses the templates to create and publish posts.

Brand guidelines

I documented the identity in a brand guidelines deck covering the logo, color palette, typography and Instagram templates, giving Vxlid a framework for representing the brand consistently as the publication grows.

Reflection

Vxlid strengthened my experience designing an identity for a social-first brand, where every story has to work within the same set of Instagram templates. It also showed me how much a visual system can shape tone: here, a deliberately serious look is what lets the humor land.

The project also reinforced an aspect of my design practice that continues through my other work: creating flexible visual systems rather than isolated assets.
