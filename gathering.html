The Gathering 2026

  • Web Design
  • Graphic Design

The Gathering is a biennial conference for film and television production designers, organized by the Production Designers Collective. For the 2026 Gathering in Spetses, Greece, I designed and built the adaptive event website, designed the prospectus deck and attendee badges, and created the custom gradient that became a signature of the event’s visual identity.

Event Website

The challenge

The website was the Gathering’s main digital home, and it had to serve very different visitors: prospective attendees deciding whether to travel to Greece, sponsors and partners evaluating the event, PDC members following along and registered attendees looking for schedules and logistics.

It also had to grow with the event. The site launched in three phases: a one-page teaser, a full site launch and a program launch. Each phase had to work on its own while content was still arriving.

The build also came with its own constraints. The PDC required the site to be built in Wix’s Classic Editor, which uses fixed-position layouts rather than fluid ones and handles mobile as a separate layout derived from the desktop design rather than as a true responsive breakpoint. That meant planning both versions together in Figma, then adjusting and checking the mobile layout page by page as the site grew across each phase.

My role

I designed and built the website, from information architecture through the launched Wix build. I also gathered much of the site’s content and wrote copy for the site where needed. The program page and homepage hero were designed by PDC leadership.

  • Information architecture and content organization
  • Content gathering, copywriting and editing
  • Adaptive layouts and visual design in Figma
  • Wix build and implementation across three launch phases

Process

Mapping the content

I started in FigJam by mapping what users needed to find and how they would move through the site based on the 2024 Gathering website. That map became the navigation: Program, About, The Island, FAQ and Register.

Designing for a phased launch

Because content arrived in stages, I structured the site so each phase could launch complete: a one-page teaser to announce important dates, a full site to announce key speakers and drive registration, and a program release closer to the event.

Making a long program easy to navigate

The program covered several days of sessions, workshops and events. I added an introductory section, with anchor-link buttons above it that jump straight to each day, and a back-to-top link at the end of the program so users could return to the top without scrolling through everything.

Accessibility review

The project’s scope didn’t allow for user testing before launch. Afterward, I audited the site against WCAG guidelines and made these fixes:

  • Wrote unique alt text for speaker portraits and sponsor logos
  • Removed duplicate content so screen reader users don’t hear the same information twice

Extending the Identity

Hero gradient

The Gathering is the PDC’s flagship event, so its look needs to feel like part of the PDC. The 2026 edition was the first since the PDC’s 2025 brand refresh, which meant the visuals from past Gatherings no longer matched the organization behind it. To bring the event in line with the new brand, I set up a Figma library of the PDC’s brand assets and components, so the website, prospectus deck and attendee badges all drew from the same source. The brand guide already included gradients for the Gathering, and I designed a new one that became the 2026 hero gradient, giving this edition its own identity within the brand.

I introduced the gradient on the prospectus cover, and it became a signature of the event: I carried it onto the website, PDC leadership used it across all of the event’s presentations and the program designer Bhumika Ravuru brought it into the printed booklet.

Prospectus deck

Before the event, the prospectus introduced the Gathering to prospective sponsors and partners and laid out the opportunities to support it. I designed the deck’s structure and layout, organizing the content so partners could quickly understand the event, its audience and the ways to get involved.

Inside the deck, I used rectangles of the gradient as recurring accents to break up text and images.

The Gathering secured six sponsors, including Warner Bros. Studios, Rosco and ShotDeck.

Attendee badges

At the event, the badges were among the first designed objects attendees held, and they helped people connect. I took them from concept through production-ready files.

My priority was legibility. I set attendee names in a medium-weight sans serif with a black-on-white color scheme for maximum contrast, and sized them to read from a short-to-medium distance so people could catch a name mid-conversation without leaning in. The custom gradient frames the front of the badge as a border, so the identity carries into the physical event without competing with the text.

Reflection

Designing, writing and building this site taught me how much of web design is content strategy, especially when content arrives in stages and requirements evolve. Next time, I would test the navigation with a few real attendees before launch and build accessibility into the build process instead of auditing afterward.
